Said to Contain

You bought the container legally. What's inside isn't.

The crate

You bought the container fair and square at the terminal auction. Behind the usual junk sits a sealed crate that is not on the manifest. That night your phone rings. The crate belongs to a cartel, and they want it back.

Give it back, and the cartel has more work for you. Take it to the customs house, and it turns out customs has a use for you too. Or keep it and find someone in town who will buy it. Whichever you pick, you find out what it costs the next time you go to work.

The work

Every lot is a sealed container, its doors chained a hand's width open. Shine your torch through the gap, guess what's inside, then bid against four rival buyers who each want something different.

Win, and the container is yours. Move the load out with the pallet jack and look over each item. The more you handle a kind of goods, the closer your guess at what it's worth.

Then sell. List things on your phone and wait for the bids to come in, or load the van and drive them to a dealer on Dock Road who pays on the spot for the right goods.

Port Calder

A small working port town in the rain. You drive everywhere yourself: the auction yard, the dealers, the customs house, and the bar on Dock Road with a back lot you only visit after dark.
